# Reference business cards — how much of the card does the company name own?

Collected to answer one question: **is the AQUAFIX wordmark on the front of the card too
small?** Same grading scheme as [`../sites/README.md`](../sites/README.md).

Reproduced side by side in Figma: [`business_card_refs`](https://www.figma.com/design/x7rCDMFviutxIJmbMHntuv).

| Grade | Means |
|---|---|
| **A** | Published brand standard or corporate policy with stated measurements |
| **B** | Real artefact, characteristics described by multiple independent sources, no spec sheet |
| **C** | Approved-vendor template — a real market artefact, but not the brand owner's own spec |
| **D** | Our reconstruction from the brand's published identity system; card itself never published |

Every measurement below is `brand mark ink width ÷ card trim width`, taken off the source
image. Card is 3.5″ wide unless stated.

---

## The measurement

| # | Card | Grade | Mark width | Mark ÷ card | Is the mark the largest thing on its side? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sotheby's Int'l Realty — front | A | 2.36″ | **67%** | yes — it is the *only* thing |
| 2 | Sotheby's Int'l Realty — back | A | — | — | no mark on the back at all |
| 3 | Douglas Elliman agent card | C | ~2.3″ | **65–69%** | yes — larger than the agent's name |
| 4 | Goldman Sachs (EMEA / US / APAC) | A | 0.65″ | **19%** | yes — ~1.3× the name, and nothing else competes |
| 5 | Buffett-style engraved calling card | B | no logo | — | the name *is* the mark |
| 6 | McKinsey & Company | D | unknown | — | unknown |
| — | **Aquafix front (current)** | — | **0.67″** | **19%** | **no — the phone number is 2.3× its type size** |

**The pattern that holds across all of them: the brand mark is the largest element on
whichever side it appears.** Two ways to satisfy that — give it the whole side (Sotheby's,
Elliman: 65–69%) or keep the whole card quiet so a small mark still wins (Goldman: 19%).
Aquafix picked Goldman's mark size and put a 17.8pt phone number next to it.

## 1–2. Sotheby's International Realty — grade A

Sources: [Identity Standards Manual 2013–14](https://peaktraining.org/Identity%20Standards.pdf),
[Sales Associate Guidelines 2018](https://peaktraining.org/Sales_Associates_Guidelines.pdf).
The strongest reference in the set — a real luxury brokerage publishing exact point sizes.

**Card:** 3.5″ × 2.125″. Domtar Solutions 100# Cover, Soft White. **Two inks only:** SIR Blue
and SIR Text Gray, lockup reversing to white.

**Front is 100% brand.** Navy field, `DBA | Sotheby's / INTERNATIONAL REALTY` lockup centred,
2.36″ wide (67% of the card). No name, no phone, no address. *"The design on the front of
the card remains the same regardless of which back-of-card design you choose."*

**Back is a flat information block**, left margin .1875″, text block 1.8255″ tall:

```
Name                Benton Sans Bold      9      / 11   SIR Blue
Professional Title  Benton Sans Regular   8.5    / 11   Text Gray
Secondary Title     Benton Sans Regular   —      / 11   Text Gray
Company Name        Benton Sans Regular   8.5    / 11   Text Gray
Address & numerals  Benton Sans Regular   8.5    / 11   Text Gray
Email & web         Benton Sans Regular   7.5*   / 11   Text Gray
Disclaimer          Benton Sans Regular   4      / 9    Text Gray
                    * 7pt if the email comes within .25″ of the edge
```

**Largest-to-smallest type ratio on the entire back: 9 ÷ 7.5 = 1.2 : 1.** There is no hero
number anywhere on the card.

**The hard floor.** *"The logo should not appear in sizes smaller than one inch in height
for the vertical Logo Lock-up and 1.25 inches in length for the horizontal Logo Lock-up."*
On a 3.5″ card, 1.25″ is **36% of the width** — and that is the absolute minimum permitted
on **any** substrate, signage included. The Aquafix lockup at 0.67″ would be roughly half
the smallest size this brand allows anywhere.

Also banned outright: marks, symbols, individual awards, colour photography, affiliate icons
on the back.

## 3. Douglas Elliman — grade C

Source: [Realty Studio Design approved-agent templates](https://www.realtystudiodesign.com/douglas-elliman-business-cards/)
(39 templates; vendor, not Elliman HQ — Elliman's own manual is behind the agent portal).

Useful *because* it is the noisy end of the market: headshots, house photos, social icons,
five contact rows, script taglines. And even here, the `DouglasElliman` wordmark runs
**65–69% of the card width and is set larger than the agent's own name.** When the discipline
goes, the brand mark's dominance is the last thing to go.

Counter-example for everything else on these cards.

## 4. Goldman Sachs — grade A

Source: [Business Card and E-mail Signature Policy, September 2017](https://goldmansachs.papirfly.com/readimage.aspx?pubid=dd859c0d-5722-42ab-bc37-c4affdbe0058&down=1)
(official; contains sample EMEA, US and APAC cards).

The one card in the set that looks superficially like Aquafix's — small mark, dense left-aligned
text — and the comparison is exactly where the problem shows.

- **Logo fixed on the right-hand side**, embossed; flat-printed versions set it in blue.
  Two-line serif lockup, 0.65″ wide = **19% of card width**, ghosted grey against cream.
- **All text left-aligned**, one block, in a fixed order: entity → office address → tel →
  email → *gap* → **name (bold)** → division → sub-division.
- **Hard cap of 8 lines** (9 in APAC), plus a regulatory footnote at ~5pt.
- Regulatory banner runs full width along the bottom edge.

**The whole card sits inside about a 1.4 : 1 type range.** The name is bold and roughly 8pt;
the logo's cap height is larger than the name's. Nothing on the card shouts, so a 19% mark
still reads as the most important object. Aquafix copies the 19% and then puts a 17.8pt
number beside it.

Banned: home addresses, private email, toll-free numbers.

## 5. Buffett-style engraved calling card — grade B

Sources: [RR Auction lot listing](https://www.rrauction.com/auctions/lot-detail/341466305678409-warren-buffett-signed-business-card/),
[Studio Burke DC, who sell the replica style](https://studioburkedc.com/products/bespoke-stationery-warren-buffett-calling-card-warren-buffett-business-card-us-size-single-colour-text).

3.5″ × 2″, 100% cotton pearl white, **hand-engraved, single-colour text, no logo, no rule, no
photo, no colour.** The name and the contact lines are the entire design.

The limit case of the same rule: when there is no mark, the *name* becomes the mark and gets
the top of the hierarchy. Buffett's card is the extreme of "nothing competes."

---

## 6. McKinsey & Company — grade D, card not published

The identity is fully documented — [Wolff Olins, 2019](https://wolffolins.com/work/mckinsey):
two bespoke typefaces (**Bower**, a serif named for Marvin Bower, drawn with Radim Peško, and
**McKinsey Sans**), a deep-blue/bright-blue palette applied at high contrast against white,
and a restacked wordmark. What is *not* published anywhere we could find is the business card
or any printed stationery — the Wolff Olins case page carries no collateral shots.

Reproduced in the Figma file as a **reconstruction only**, from the published system. Do not
cite it as evidence.

---

## No public artefact — a finding, not a gap

**BlackRock.** No employee card is publicly indexed. Every image result for "BlackRock
business card" is either (a) an unrelated company — Blackrock Industries, Blackrock
Automation, a RockDesign template line called "Blackrocks" — or (b) speculative portfolio
work on Behance/Dribbble. The published BlackRock identity is a black wordmark on white, one
colour, with a white-on-dark-blue variant; the wordmark itself is drawn wide and heavy, which
is very likely what read as "giant" — that is the *logo's own proportions*, not its size on a
card. Worth checking whether the card that prompted this was actually BlackRock's.

**Bridgewater Associates.** Same. No published identity, no case study, no collateral. A
notoriously private firm; the only public assets are logo scrapes on Brandfetch.

Both are recorded here so they don't get re-searched.

## What this says about the Aquafix front

```
                     brand mark ÷ card width
  0%        20%        40%        60%        80%
  |----------|----------|----------|----------|
  ▓▓▓▓ 19%   Goldman Sachs        · nothing on the card is bigger
  ▓▓▓▓ 19%   AQUAFIX (current)    · the phone is 2.3× bigger  ◄── the outlier
  ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓ 36%             · Sotheby's absolute floor, any substrate
  ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓ 65%  Douglas Elliman
  ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓ 67% Sotheby's Int'l Realty front
```

Measured off `business_card/lib.typ`:

| Element (front) | Type size | vs. wordmark |
|---|---|---|
| Phone `(503) 555-0148` | 74px @300dpi = **17.8pt** | **2.3×** |
| `AQUAFIX` wordmark | 32.68px = **7.8pt** | 1.0× |
| `aquafix.com` | 24px = 5.8pt | 0.73× |
| `24 HOURS · 7 DAYS` | 18px = 4.3pt | 0.55× |

The wordmark is not small in absolute terms — 7.8pt type and a 0.67″ lockup is Goldman
territory. It is small *relative to its own card*, and that is the thing no reference in this
set does. Aquafix's front is running the trade-services convention (phone number as hero, as
on a Mr. Rooter van) inside prestige-card styling, and the two conventions disagree about
what the biggest object should be.

## What was built

The **Sotheby's split**. Front carries the lock-up at 59% of the trim width plus the promise
line, and nothing else. Phone, hours, licence, site and serving radius all moved to the back,
which is now the information side — four contact rows, the guarantee box, and a terms strip.
The mark came off the back with them: the front is 100% brand, so a second mark had nothing
left to do.

The rejected alternative was to keep both on the front and flatten the type instead (phone
17.8pt → ~11pt, wordmark → ~13pt), which is the Goldman shape. It survives as frame 10 in the
Figma file.

Cost of the split: the card no longer reads as glance-and-dial. [`../sites/README.md`](../sites/README.md)
justifies the hero phone on the **landing page**, where the reader is standing in water. A card
is handed over after that conversation, so the argument does not carry across.
